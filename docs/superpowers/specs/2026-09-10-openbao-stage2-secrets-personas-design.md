# OpenBao Stage 2 — the repoint, and who may read what

Parent design: [`2026-09-02-openbao-store-of-record-design.md`](2026-09-02-openbao-store-of-record-design.md).
That document specifies Stage 2 and leaves it unplanned; this one closes the two
gaps that stop a plan being written, then hands off to `superpowers:writing-plans`.

## Why this exists

Stage 1 shipped the foundation — lineage, rehydrate-at-boot, JWT auth, the PKI
under the offline root, snapshot mirroring, a green restore drill. Stage 2, the
repoint of the `ClusterSecretStore` onto OpenBao, was carved out of that plan on
purpose ("*without repointing the ClusterSecretStore — that is Stage 2*") and
gated on Stage 1's exit criteria. Those criteria are met: the drill ran green
(`414f7d10`) and now runs weekly. **Stage 2 never got a plan, so nothing
executed it.**

The consequence is visible on the running platform, and it is the shape of a
door with no key:

| Built by Stage 1 | State today |
|---|---|
| `auth/jwt/aws-0/role/external-secrets`, bound to `system:serviceaccount:security:external-secrets`, audience `openbao` | `token_policies = [default]` — authenticates, can read nothing |
| `app` namespace, `secret/` kv-v2 mount, AppRole, `app.hcl` | no consumer anywhere in the cluster |
| `oidc/` mount with `groups_claim = "groups"` (ADR-0034) | one coarse `admin` group; no policy reaches any secret |
| `ClusterSecretStore` on both clusters | still `aws: SecretsManager` / `gcpsm` |

Every application secret on this platform is still read from a cloud managed
store. Twenty-six `ExternalSecret` documents point there.

## The two gaps this design closes

The parent design specifies the mount, the migration tool, the repoint and the
records. It does not answer:

1. **Where do application secrets live**, as distinct from platform component
   secrets, and
2. **who may read and write them** — it lists human OIDC auth as a follow-up,
   which shipped later as ADR-0034 with a single `admin` group and no grant over
   any secret path.

## Target

### Two mounts, both in the root namespace

| Mount | Grammar | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| `platform/` | `platform/<component>/<name>` — the parent design's grammar, mapping one-to-one onto ADR-0023's dash names (`harbor-admin-password` → `platform/harbor/admin-password`) | platform admins |
| `apps/` | `apps/<app>/<key>` | the owning app's group |

Root namespace is load-bearing rather than incidental. A policy binds only
within the namespace it is created in, and the `oidc/` mount, the identity
groups and every existing policy are in root. Putting the mounts anywhere else
reintroduces a cross-namespace identity problem that has no clean answer in
OpenBao — which is exactly the wall the pre-existing `app` namespace hit.

Two mounts rather than one because ESO's `vault` provider takes a single mount
per store (`spec.provider.vault.path`), so the split is what lets the two
audiences carry different policies without path-prefix gymnastics inside one
policy document.

### Personas

Authorisation flows through the `groups` claim that ADR-0034 established and
that this session repaired (the role requested only `openid`, so no claim
arrived at all — fixed in #2012).

| ZITADEL role | OpenBao external group | Policies |
|---|---|---|
| `admin` | `openbao-admin` (exists) | `admin`, `pki-admin` (exist) **+ `secrets-admin`** — full CRUD over both mounts |
| `app-<name>` | `openbao-app-<name>` | `app-<name>` — CRUD on `apps/data/<name>/*` plus `metadata`/`delete`/`undelete`; **no `destroy`** |

`destroy` stays out of the per-app policy for the reason `app.hcl` already
gives: a compromised credential must not be able to erase secret history.

**Per-app policies and groups are generated by `for_each` over an explicit app
list, not by identity templating.** Templating (`{{identity.groups.names.…}}`)
collapses N policies into one, but the grant becomes invisible — it cannot be
read off a plan diff, and a mistake in the template silently widens every app's
reach at once. With `for_each`, onboarding an app is a list entry plus a ZITADEL
role, and every grant appears in `tofu plan`. The cost is real and accepted:
adding an app touches Terraform.

The list is seeded from the `App` claims the platform runs today — `app-wizard`,
`xplane-image-gallery`, `xplane-podinfo`, `xplane-openwebui`, `podinfo`,
`xplane-pev2` — but a group is created only for apps that actually hold secrets,
not for all six on principle. An app with no secrets gets no group and no
prefix; the plan decides the day-one set by reading which claims consume an
`ExternalSecret`.

### Machines read, humans write

The `external-secrets` JWT role gets a **read-only** policy over
`platform/data/*` and `apps/data/*` (plus the `metadata` paths ESO lists). It
gets no write capability of any kind.

This is the property that makes per-app ownership mean something. If ESO could
write, any workload able to shape an `ExternalSecret` could launder a value into
another app's prefix.

### The stores

Two `ClusterSecretStore` objects per cluster, both `vault` provider, both
authenticating with the ESO ServiceAccount token against `jwt/<cluster>` role
`external-secrets` — the role Stage 1 already created.

### What stays in the managed store

The bootstrap tier, unchanged from the parent design: CA chain, OpenBao server
TLS cert and key, root token, recovery keys, intermediate CA bundle — the five
things read before OpenBao has an API, plus the seal key as a sixth item that is
an AWS resource rather than a secret. Flux's GitHub App key moves to OpenBao, as
the parent design decided; that decision is inherited, not reopened.

## Migration

Non-destructive and reversible at every step:

1. `scripts/secret-store.sh` gains `--store openbao` for `check`, `lint`, `seed`,
   and a `migrate` that copies managed store → OpenBao, never overwriting and
   never deleting the source. The script today has `check`, `lint`, `seed` and
   `migrate-aws`.
2. Both stores exist side by side while `ExternalSecret` documents are repointed.
3. Migrated managed-store entries are deleted **by hand** after a grace period,
   with the command recorded in the PR.

At no point is the old store removed before the new one is proven for that key.

## Records

- **New ADR-0036** — *per-app secret ownership through ZITADEL groups, generated
  per app rather than templated*. Rejected alternatives: identity templating (one
  policy, invisible grants); flat tiers of reader/writer/admin over the whole
  store (a writer reaches every app's secrets); per-app OpenBao namespaces (the
  cross-namespace identity problem again, for no gain over a path prefix).
- The parent design's Stage 2 record list stands: ADR-0023 note, ADR-0025
  amendment, constitution §3.2 wording, costs page.
- `.doc-claims.yaml` gains claims for the two mount names.
- `CLAUDE.md` "Namespace layout" — today it says `app` is the only namespace and
  holds the only kv-v2 mount. Both mounts land in root, and the `app` namespace
  becomes dead weight; the design's position is that it should be **removed**
  rather than left as a decoy, since nothing consumes it.

## Success criteria

1. A human in `openbao-app-<name>` can read and write `apps/<name>/*` in the UI
   and is denied on another app's prefix.
2. A human in `openbao-admin` can read and write both mounts.
3. ESO reconciles every repointed `ExternalSecret` with `Ready=True`, reading
   from OpenBao.
4. The ESO token is denied on a write to either mount.
5. `secret-store.sh check --store aws` lists exactly the bootstrap tier.
6. A from-scratch deploy reaches every Flux Kustomization `Ready=True` with no
   manual seeding beyond the bootstrap tier.
7. `./scripts/validate-manifests.sh` reports `Invalid: 0, Skipped: 0`.

## Risks

- **A repoint that half-lands leaves apps without secrets.** Mitigated by
  migrating copy-first and repointing per `ExternalSecret`, not per cluster.
- **The `groups` claim is fragile in a way that looks like an authorisation
  bug.** Three things must hold: ZITADEL `projectRoleAssertion` true, the
  `groupsFromRoles` Action present, and the OIDC role requesting the `groups`
  scope. The third was broken until #2012 and produced
  `claim "email" not found in token`, not a permission error. Any per-app group
  that appears to grant nothing should be diagnosed against these three first.
- **A ZITADEL role must exist before its OpenBao group is useful**, and a human
  does not exist in ZITADEL until first login. Onboarding is therefore two-sided
  and cannot be fully driven from this repository.
- **Deleting the `app` namespace destroys its mount.** It holds no data today;
  this must be confirmed at execution time rather than assumed.

## Out of scope

- Dynamic secrets and credential issuance. Both mounts are static kv-v2.
- Anything about the GCP cluster beyond keeping its `ClusterSecretStore` at
  parity with AWS.
- Revisiting the bootstrap tier or the seal design.
